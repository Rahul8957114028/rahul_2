Field Guide: 4 Early Visual Warning Signs of Slope Movement

Landslides rarely happen completely without warning. Look for these subtle physical indicators:

1. Ground & Wall Fissures

New hairline cracks appearing in road asphalt, paved driveways, concrete patio slabs, or vertical brick retaining walls that widen over days.

Action: Mark crack edges with chalk and check if gap expands after rainfall.

2. Tilted Trees & Poles

Pine trees, wooden utility poles, or boundary fence posts tilting downhill or developing "pistol-butt" curved trunk bases indicate active creeping topsoil.

Action: Report tilted power lines to Forest and Electricity departments immediately.

3. Murky Spring Runoff

Mountain drinking water springs suddenly turning dark reddish-brown with heavy clay silt, or dry hillside ground suddenly developing wet seepages.

Action: Indicates subsurface piping erosion. High risk of localized slope blow-out.

4. Low Freight Rumblings

A continuous low, dull freight-train rumbling vibration in the ground, accompanied by loud snapping roots or boulders clacking together in gullies.

Action: Failure in progress! Run perpendicular to the slope immediately!

Household Action Protocol

Phase 1: Before Heavy Rains

Clear roadside drains of fallen pine needles and stones. Ensure water from roof downspouts discharges away from the slope crest.

Phase 2: During Advisory Warning

Keep cellphones fully charged. Sleep in bedrooms on the uphill side of the house (away from outer slope drop-offs).

Phase 3: Immediate Evacuation

Grab your prepared 72h pack. Lock front door only if time permits. Proceed immediately to designated safe shelter. Do not turn back for pets or valuables.
